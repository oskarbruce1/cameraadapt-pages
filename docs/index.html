What's inside CameraAdapt?

Why CameraAdapt?

The fastest way from "how did they shoot that?" to actually shooting it.

Stop guessing settings

Every reference look carries verified EXIF from the original frame. Facts are stated plainly; anything estimated is marked as an estimate, never dressed up as certainty.

Built for your gear, not a full-frame ideal

Most tutorials assume a full-frame body and an f/1.4 prime. CameraAdapt works with the crop sensor and the kit lens you actually own.

From photo to shot in seconds

Pick a look, read your settings, go and shoot. No menus, no manuals, no three-hour rabbit hole on a forum from 2011.

Any photo

Not just our library — any photo you like

Upload a shot you found on Instagram, a frame from a friend, or something from your own camera roll. CameraAdapt reads the EXIF where it exists, estimates the rest, and always tells you which is which — estimates are hedged, never stated as fact.

Questions, answered

Everything people ask before they download it.

Which cameras does it support?

111 bodies and 174 lenses — Canon, Nikon, Sony, Fujifilm, Panasonic, OM System and more, including fixed-lens compacts like the Fujifilm X100 and Ricoh GR III, which skip the lens step automatically.

Do I need to know photography already?

No. That's the point. CameraAdapt gives you the numbers to dial in and the order to do it in, in your camera's own language. If you can find aperture priority on your body, you can use it.

Where do the reference photos come from?

100 reference photos across ten moods, each with intact, verified EXIF from the original file — the real aperture, shutter, ISO and focal length, not a guess at what they might have been.

What if my gear can't match the shot?

It tells you. Every result carries a match quality — a clean match, or a close match with the trade-offs spelled out and a workaround for each one. It never pretends your kit can do something it can't.

Does it edit the photo for me?

It gives you the recipe, not a filter. Exposure, highlights, shadows, blacks, clarity, vibrance and temperature, written in your editor's own controls — and exported as a Lightroom .xmp, a Capture One .costyle, or a .cube LUT for DaVinci Resolve, Premiere and Photoshop.

What does it cost?

CameraAdapt is free to download and walks you through a real adaptation on your own gear when you first open it. Adapting looks after that needs Pro: $14.99/month or $49.99/year, both starting with a 3-day free trial. Prices vary by region.

Do I need an account?

No. There's no sign-up, no login and no password. Your gear and your saved looks live on your device.
